Models

Heat Index

heat-index-rothfuszNo standard, so Explore only2 inputs

The Heat Index, also called the apparent temperature, expresses how hot it feels when humidity is taken into account alongside air temperature. It is the index used in United States weather warnings, and it is deliberately simple: two inputs, one number, five risk categories.

Environmental factors

Input Applicability Chart axis
Air temperature 27 °C and above, with no upper bound 20 to 50 °C
Relative humidity No bound of its own, beyond the 0 to 100 % its kind implies 0 to 100 %
The lower bound is not a formality. Heat Index is defined for warm conditions only it says nothing about a cool or cold day. The bound the tool gates on is 27 °C, the single bound the calculation library publishes as HEAT_INDEX_ROTHFUSZ_LIMITS; there is no upper bound and humidity carries none at all. An entered air temperature below 27 °C is out of range and the model is not run.
The two columns differ on purpose. The chart axes are declared by the model file, 20 to 50 °C against 0 to 100 %, so that the banded field is drawn the way a published heat-index table looks. An axis range is a viewport, not a limit on what may be entered. Both axes had to be declared here precisely because applicability could answer for neither: it is minimum-only for air temperature and absent for humidity. Verified at 4d6f6df.
For cold conditions the deployed tool offers Wind Chill, and UTCI covers the whole range. Neither is registered in rewrite/v1, so neither can be reached there yet.

Personal factors

None. The index assumes a person in shade, so it does not account for direct sun, and it does not account for wind. Both make a real difference outdoors, which is why UTCI exists.

Outputs and risk categories

The index returns a single apparent temperature, classified into five categories:

Category Broadly means
No risk Conditions are not a heat hazard.
Caution Fatigue is possible with prolonged exposure or activity.
Extreme caution Heat cramps or exhaustion are possible; heat stroke possible with prolonged activity.
Danger Heat cramps or exhaustion likely; heat stroke possible.
Extreme danger Heat stroke highly likely.

Charts

Heat Index has one chart: a banded field of air temperature against relative humidity, the familiar shape of a published heat-index table. Its axes are fixed to those two quantities, since they are the only inputs.

Applicability

Heat Index is an empirical fit, not a heat-balance model. It is reliable in the warm, humid conditions it was fitted for and should not be extrapolated beyond them the tool gates entry at 27 °C. It also does not represent anyone in particular: it assumes shade, still air and a typical adult.

If you need to assess a specific workplace rather than a general public warning, PHS is the appropriate model. Heat Index is a communication tool, not an occupational assessment.

Where to find it

In Explore only. It belongs to no building standard, so it has no compliance workspace and no pass/fail verdict.
