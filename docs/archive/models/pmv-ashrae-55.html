Models

PMV / PPD (ASHRAE 55)

pmv-ppd-ashraeASHRAE 55:2023ExploreDefault model

The Predicted Mean Vote is Fanger's heat-balance model. It predicts the average thermal sensation a large group of people would report in a given environment, on a seven-point scale from −3 (cold) to +3 (hot), and the Predicted Percentage Dissatisfied follows from it. This page describes the model as evaluated under ANSI/ASHRAE Standard 55.

Six primary factors affect thermal comfort. Four describe the environment air temperature, mean radiant temperature, air speed and humidity and two describe the person: metabolic rate and clothing insulation.

PMV uses all six.

In rewrite/v1 this is the default model: ADR-0002 decision 74 made the registry the tool's one display order, and this model heads it. It is reached at /standard/ashrae-55/pmv-ppd-ashrae and from Explore, and defaults to 25 °C air and radiant temperature, 0.1 m/s air speed, 50 % relative humidity, 1.1 met and 0.5 clo. Its result table shows PMV, PPD and a compliance verdict read as Yes or No. Two input calculators attach to its rows: solar gain on mean radiant temperature, and both clothing calculators on clothing insulation. A PMV of exactly 0.5 reads as Neutral, because this model's sensation bands are right-inclusive, where ISO's are not. Verified at 4d6f6df.

Environmental factors

Dry-bulb air temperature

The temperature of the air around the occupant, measured with a shielded sensor so that radiation does not bias the reading. It is the input most people reach for first, and the quantity the psychrometric chart puts on its horizontal axis. Accepted from 10 to 40 °C.

Entering operative temperature instead

The temperature mode selector lets you supply a single operative temperature rather than air and radiant temperature separately. Operative temperature combines the two, weighted by the air speed, and is convenient when you have one measured value and no way to separate them. Switching the mode changes which fields the input panel shows and which quantity carries the chart's temperature axis. PMV defaults to separate air and radiant temperature.

Mean radiant temperature

The area-weighted average temperature of the surfaces surrounding the occupant, accepted over the same 10 to 40 °C range. A cold window in winter or a sunlit wall in summer moves it several degrees away from the air temperature, and the body responds to that difference as strongly as it responds to the air itself.

Where the occupant sits in direct sun, the solar gain input modifier calculates the increase in mean radiant temperature from the sun's position and the glazing properties and adds it to the value you entered.

Air speed

Air movement carries heat away from the skin, so raising the air speed makes a warm environment feel cooler. The model works with the relative air speed the movement of air past the body, which includes the air the occupant stirs up by moving. Accepted from 0 to 2 m/s.

Self-generated air speed

If you have measured the still-air speed in the room and want the model to account for the occupant's own movement, enable the measured air speed input modifier. It derives relative air speed from the measured value and the metabolic rate:

v_relative = v_measured + 0.3 × (met − 1)

Occupant control of air speed

PMV under ASHRAE 55 carries an option no other model has: whether occupants can adjust the air speed locally, with a desk fan or an operable ceiling fan. The standard permits higher air speeds where they can, so the option changes the upper bounds applied to the air speed.

In rewrite/v1 the option is off by default, matching the deployed CBE tool rather than the calculation library's own default, which is on. An earlier version of this page said local control is assumed present unless you say otherwise; that is the opposite of what the tool does.
With the option off, v1 follows the library's air-speed rule and not the deployed tool's. The library applies up to three upper bounds to the relative air speed, taken at ISO 7726's air-speed-weighted operative temperature, and only below 0.7 clo and 1.3 met; the bound is reported against the air speed as entered. The deployed tool instead checks the entered air speed against a single limit clamped to 0.2–0.8 m/s, at the plain mean of air and radiant temperature, at every clothing and activity level. That difference is deliberately not ported, and is the kind of thing the acceptance pass in issue #30 exists to record. Verified at 4d6f6df.

This is the only part of the current tool that corresponds to the "fans during heatwaves" material in the previous documentation; there is no separate fan or heatwave feature in this build.

Humidity

Humidity limits how much the body can cool itself by evaporation. Its influence on the predicted vote is modest across the ordinary indoor range and grows as conditions get warmer. Relative humidity is accepted across the full 0 to 100 % range.

The tool accepts humidity in five equivalent forms: relative humidity, humidity ratio, dew point temperature, wet-bulb temperature and vapour pressure. All five describe the same state of the air you enter whichever you have and the tool converts it. Relative humidity is the default.

Personal factors

Metabolic rate

The rate at which the body produces heat, in met, where 1 met is a seated person at rest. Typing at a desk is about 1.1 met and walking slowly about 2 met. Accepted from 1 to 4 met. Because the model is a heat balance, the metabolic rate sets how much heat must be lost for the occupant to be comfortable, which makes it one of the most influential inputs.

Clothing insulation

The thermal resistance of what the occupant is wearing, in clo, where 1 clo is roughly a business suit. Under ASHRAE 55 the tool accepts up to 1.5 clo. Clothing is the most effective thing an occupant can adjust: half a clo moves the comfortable temperature by several degrees, which is why clothing policy is often a cheaper route to comfort than changing the setpoint.

Three tools help you arrive at a value:

  • The clothing ensemble builder assembles a garment list by body zone and totals the insulation.
  • The morning clothing estimate modifier predicts what occupants are likely to be wearing from the outdoor temperature at 6 a.m.
  • The dynamic clothing modifier reduces the entered insulation to account for movement at the current metabolic rate. Unlike the previous version of this tool, this correction is applied only when you switch the modifier on.

Outputs

Output Meaning
PMV Predicted mean vote on the seven-point sensation scale, from −3 to +3.
PPD Predicted percentage of occupants dissatisfied with the thermal environment.
Sensation category Cold, Cool, Slightly Cool, Neutral, Slightly Warm, Warm or Hot.
Compliance verdict Whether the point falls inside the standard's acceptable PMV range.

Charts

Four charts are available: the psychrometric chart with the comfort zone, the dynamic field chart, heat loss against air temperature, and SET. The comfort zone on the psychrometric chart is the region where the predicted mean vote stays within the acceptable limits either side of neutral, drawn for the metabolic rate, clothing and air speed you entered so it moves when you change them.

The bounds this model gates on are the ones the calculation library publishes as ASHRAE_55_LIMITS: air temperature and mean radiant temperature 10 to 40 °C, relative air speed 0 to 2 m/s, metabolic rate 1 to 4 met and clothing insulation 0 to 1.5 clo. ASHRAE 55 bounds neither the vapour pressure nor the PMV output, so unlike ISO 7730 there is no bound on either. These are not the same thing as the chart axes, which this model declares separately and which run wider on purpose.

Applicability

Before returning a verdict the tool checks the inputs against the standard's applicability range, and reports a point outside it as out of range rather than as a pass or a fail. Values are still calculated and shown, but a standard that does not claim to cover a state cannot be used to certify it.

Difference from the ISO 7730 variant

PMV (ASHRAE 55) PMV (ISO 7730)
Occupant control of air speed Available as an option, off by default No such setting
Clothing applicability Up to 1.5 clo Up to 2 clo
Banding on its workspace Acceptable / not acceptable The seven sensation categories
Operative temperature ASHRAE formulation ISO formulation

The two are close but not interchangeable, which is why the single old documentation/pmv page becomes two.

Where to find it

On the ASHRAE 55 workspace, where it is the default model, and in Explore, where the axes can be chosen and the bands edited. The verdict shown on the compliance workspace is a compliance statement; the same model in Explore with edited bands is not.
