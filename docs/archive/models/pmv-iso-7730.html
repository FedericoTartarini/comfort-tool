PMV / PPD (ISO 7730)

pmv-ppd-isoISO 7730:2025Standard pageExplore

The same Fanger heat-balance model described on the ASHRAE 55 page, evaluated under ISO 7730. The equations agree; what differs is the applicability range, how the result is banded, and which provisions the standard allows.

Six primary factors affect thermal comfort. Four describe the environment air temperature, mean radiant temperature, air speed and humidity and two describe the person: metabolic rate and clothing insulation.

PMV uses all six, and the descriptions of each factor on the ASHRAE page apply here unchanged. This page covers what is specific to the ISO treatment.

In rewrite/v1 this model declares ISO 7730:2025, is reached at /standard/iso-7730/pmv-ppd-iso and from Explore, and defaults to 25 °C air and radiant temperature, 0.1 m/s air speed, 50 % relative humidity, 1.1 met and 0.5 clo. Its result table shows PMV and PPD only, with no compliance column. Its comfort zones are ISO categories A, B and C, nested, and the category bins are strict at both ends, so a PMV of exactly 0.5 falls in category C. One input calculator attaches here, the custom ensemble on clothing insulation. Verified at 4d6f6df.

How it differs from the ASHRAE variant

ISO 7730 ASHRAE 55
Occupant control of air speed No such setting Available as an option, off by default; with it off, up to three upper bounds apply to the relative air speed
Clothing applicability Up to 2 clo Up to 1.5 clo
Chart banding on its workspace The seven sensation categories Acceptable / not acceptable
Comfort boundary drawn Categories A, B and C, drawn as nested zones The standard's acceptable PMV limits
Operative temperature ISO formulation ASHRAE formulation
The elevated-air-speed cooling effect belongs to the ASHRAE model. Do not read an ISO result as though a fan had been accounted for.

Environmental factors

Air temperature is accepted from 10 to 30 °C and mean radiant temperature from 10 to 40 °C. Relative air speed is accepted from 0 to 1 m/s, metabolic rate from 0.8 to 4 met and clothing insulation from 0 to 2 clo. Water vapour pressure, which is derived from the temperature and the humidity rather than entered, is accepted up to 2700 Pa. Relative humidity itself carries no bound of its own beyond the 0 to 100 % its kind implies. Humidity can be entered in any of the five modes relative humidity, humidity ratio, dew point, wet bulb or vapour pressure and temperature in separate or operative mode.

These are the bounds the calculation library publishes as ISO_7730_LIMITS, which is what gates an entered value. They are narrower than this model's chart axes, which run to 40 °C and 2 m/s: a chart deliberately draws the field around the standard, and an axis range is not an applicability bound. ISO also gates the output, accepting a PMV between −2 and +2; a result outside that is reported as a violation beside the result rather than stopping the calculation.

Air speed is the relative air speed, so the measured-air-speed modifier applies the same correction for the occupant's own movement as it does on the ASHRAE model.

Personal factors

Metabolic rate is accepted from 1 to 4 met. Clothing insulation extends to 2 clo, wider than ASHRAE 55 allows the main practical difference between the two, since a heavily dressed occupant may be assessable here and out of range there.

All four input modifiers are available: measured air speed, morning clothing estimate, dynamic clothing and solar gain.

Outputs

Output Meaning
PMV Predicted mean vote, −3 to +3.
PPD Predicted percentage dissatisfied.
Sensation category Cold, Cool, Slightly Cool, Neutral, Slightly Warm, Warm or Hot.

Banding and the comfort boundary

ISO 7730 does not define a single acceptable/unacceptable band the way ASHRAE 55 does, so the workspace bands the chart by the seven sensation categories and treats Neutral as the comfort criterion. The boundary lines drawn on the chart are the edges of that category.

Categories of thermal environment

ISO 7730 describes categories of thermal environment commonly A, B and C with progressively tighter PMV limits. The tool evaluates at the category B expectation, which is the ordinary design case and the reason this model is named as it is. For a different category, compare the reported PMV against that category's limit directly.

Edition

In rewrite/v1 the model declares the ISO 7730:2025 edition, which is the calculation library's default, and the library's kernel follows that edition's Annex D (ADR-0002 decision 6). The library treats the 2005 and 2025 editions as specifying identical equations and limits, so pinning one does not change a result; it changes which edition the tool names. The edition appears in the result table's caption and in the input summary on a downloaded image. An earlier version of this page said the 2005 edition was pinned deliberately, which described the archived interface. Verified at 4d6f6df.

Applicability

Inputs outside the standard's stated range are marked and reported as out of range rather than given a verdict.

Where to find it

On the ISO 7730 workspace, which offers this model only, and in Explore.
