Frequently asked questions

Choosing a method and understanding what a result means.

Should I use PMV or adaptive comfort?

Start with the assessment context. PMV uses environmental and personal inputs; the ASHRAE adaptive method is for eligible occupant-controlled naturally conditioned spaces. Read each method’s eligibility criteria before comparing their results.

Source: pythermalcomfort model documentation.

Why can ASHRAE and ISO PMV give different results?

The selected method and its air-speed treatment matter. The ASHRAE implementation includes a SET-based cooling-effect step for elevated air speed. Also check the input assumptions, units, clothing adjustments and standard edition.

Source: pythermalcomfort model documentation.

Why does PPD not reach zero?

PPD describes dissatisfaction within a group rather than a promise that every person is comfortable. The PMV–PPD relationship has a minimum of about 5% at neutral PMV.

Source: pythermalcomfort model documentation.

Does an uncomfortable result mean the model is out of range?

No. A model can apply to a case and predict discomfort. Check the model’s applicability separately from its acceptance criterion. Do not treat a chart colour or a release-specific status label as proof that these checks are identical.

Source: pythermalcomfort model documentation.

Why does adaptive comfort have fewer inputs?

Its empirical relationship uses the outdoor climate rather than the PMV heat-balance inputs. Clothing and activity still constrain whether the method is appropriate even though they are not direct parameters of the model call.

Source: pythermalcomfort model documentation.

Can I use Heat Index as an indoor comfort verdict?

Heat Index describes apparent heat from temperature and humidity. It does not replace PMV or adaptive comfort, and its category does not establish an individual’s safety.

Source: pythermalcomfort model documentation.

Is the chart axis the model’s valid input range?

No. A chart is a viewport and may extend beyond applicability. Check the method documentation and the target app’s applicability reporting.

Source: pythermalcomfort model documentation.

Why does the archived guide show different models or controls?

The archived guide describes an earlier interface. Controls and model availability can differ between releases. The rewrite/v1 pages focus on model concepts; historical walkthroughs remain labelled Archived.

Where are the walkthroughs and screenshots?

They remain in the archive. New screenshots, routes, defaults and control instructions are deferred until a target application commit and its calculation dependency have been confirmed.

How do Compare, Time-series, calculators and sharing work in my build?

Those details vary between versions. This model-first guide does not claim the archived behaviour is present or absent in the latest app. Use release-specific documentation once it has been verified.
