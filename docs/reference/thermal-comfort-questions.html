Reference

Thermal comfort questions

Questions that come up repeatedly when people use this tool about which model to pick, why two models disagree, and what the interface is telling you when it refuses to give an answer.

This page is about interpretation. For where each model comes from, see References and citations; for how to drive the interface, see Help.

Choosing a model

Which model should I use?

Start from the environment, not the index:

SituationModelWhere
A conditioned indoor space, assessed against a standardPMV / PPDASHRAE 55 or ISO 7730
A naturally ventilated space where occupants open windowsAdaptiveASHRAE 55 or EN 16798-1
Hot work how long someone can safely stayPHSISO 7933
Outdoors, full climate rangeUTCIExplore
Communicating hot-weather risk to the publicHeat Index or HumidexExplore
Communicating cold-wind riskWind ChillExplore

Which standard applies is usually decided for you by the jurisdiction, the client or the certification scheme. Where you have a free choice, the compliance workspaces are the ones that give a verdict; Explore gives you a number and leaves the judgement to you.

Why are UTCI, Heat Index, Humidex and Wind Chill only in Explore?

Because none of them belongs to a building standard, so there is no criterion to pass or fail. They return a value and a stress or discomfort category, and that is the whole output. Putting them on a compliance workspace would imply a verdict the models cannot give.

These four are also the easiest models to misuse, because they are simple enough to apply anywhere and each one silently assumes conditions you may not have. Read the validity section on the model page before quoting a number from any of them. A heat-stress index used outside its range returns a confident, wrong answer.

Why is Adaptive not available in Explore?

The adaptive comfort band is defined by the standard as a function of outdoor temperature. Explore lets you edit band edges, which is useful for investigation but would misrepresent a model whose bands are the standard's whole content. The two adaptive models are therefore offered on their compliance workspaces only.

When two models disagree

Why do PMV (ASHRAE 55) and PMV (ISO 7730) give different answers for the same inputs?

Because they are different calculations, not two labels on one. The PMV equation is shared, but ASHRAE 55 first computes a cooling effect from elevated air speed, subtracts it from both the air and mean radiant temperatures, and then evaluates PMV with air speed set to 0.1 m/s. ISO 7730 applies Fanger's original formulation with no such adjustment.

At still air the two agree closely. The gap opens as air speed rises which is exactly the condition people most often want to model.

They also differ in what they will accept at all:

LimitASHRAE 55ISO 7730:2005
Air temperature10 to 40 °C10 to 30 °C
Mean radiant temperature10 to 40 °C10 to 40 °C
Relative air speed0 to 2 m/s0 to 1 m/s
Metabolic rate1 to 4 met0.8 to 4 met
Clothing insulation0 to 1.5 clo0 to 2 clo
Resulting PMVno bound−2 to +2

The last row surprises people. ISO 7730 bounds its own output: a case that produces a PMV beyond ±2 is outside the model's applicability even when every input was legal. ASHRAE has no equivalent rule, so the same room can return a number on one workspace and nothing on the other.

Only ASHRAE offers an air-speed control option. Why?

ASHRAE 55 imposes an upper limit on air speed that varies with operative temperature when occupants cannot control the air movement themselves a ceiling fan someone can switch off is treated differently from a draught they cannot. That rule exists only in ASHRAE, so the control appears only on the ASHRAE PMV model.

Which humidity input should I use?

Whichever one you actually measured. The five modes relative humidity, humidity ratio, dew-point temperature, wet-bulb temperature and vapour pressure are alternative ways of stating the same moisture content, and the tool converts between them. Entering a converted value you calculated by hand adds a rounding step for no benefit.

Air temperature or operative temperature?

Operative temperature combines air and mean radiant temperature into one figure. Use it when you have a single measured or specified operative value; use the air-temperature mode when you have air and radiant temperatures separately, which is the more informative case. A large gap between the two a cold window, a sunlit floor, a radiant panel is exactly the information operative mode averages away.

Reading the result

What does “Out of range” mean?

It means the model does not apply to the case you entered, so no number is given. It is not a failure verdict. A model fitted within an envelope of conditions returns nothing meaningful outside it, and showing a value there would be worse than showing none.

One caveat on PMV. On the two PMV models, Out of range is currently also shown when the inputs are perfectly valid but the resulting PMV falls outside the acceptable comfort band that is, for a room that is simply too warm or too cold. Those are different findings: one means “the model cannot answer”, the other means “the model answered, and the answer is non-compliant”. The adaptive models distinguish them correctly. Until PMV does the same, check your inputs against the limits above before reading Out of range as an applicability problem.

PPD never goes below 5%. Is that a bug?

No. PPD is derived from PMV by a fixed curve whose minimum is 5% at PMV = 0. The model's position is that in any group of people, in any environment, about one person in twenty will be dissatisfied. There is no combination of inputs that removes it.

Why does the result change when I turn on an input modifier?

Because a modifier changes the value the model sees. The base input you typed is stored separately and stays visible; the calculation runs on the modified value. Modifiers apply in a fixed order and chain into each other see Input modifiers.

The previous version corrected clothing for movement automatically. This one does not.

Correct. Dynamic clothing is now an explicit modifier you switch on. If you are comparing a result against one from the older tool, that difference is a likely cause.

About this build

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. Every calculation runs in your browser. There is no account, no server-side session and no stored history. A share link carries your inputs inside the link itself rather than uploading them.

Why is there no Share button in Time-series?

A share link encodes one set of inputs, which cannot represent a multi-segment scenario. Rather than hand out a link that silently drops the scenario, the Time-series workspace offers none. Export the charts instead.

Can I upload a weather file or a CSV?

Not in this version. There is no file import anywhere in the tool; inputs are entered by hand, and Time-series scenarios are built segment by segment.

What happened to the fans-during-heatwaves feature?

It is not present in this version. The nearest live capability is the air-speed control option on PMV (ASHRAE 55), which is a different thing: it applies the standard's air-speed limits, it does not model a fan during a heat event.

Why do the charts differ from one model to another?

Each model declares its own charts, so the set changes with the model rather than being a fixed menu. Most models offer one or two. Earlier documentation described a fixed set of five chart types; that is no longer how the tool works.
