Models

Adaptive comfort (ASHRAE 55)

adaptive-ashraeASHRAE 55 workspaceNot in Explore

The adaptive model applies to naturally conditioned spaces buildings where occupants can open windows and are free to adapt their clothing and activity. Rather than balancing heat flows, it predicts the comfortable indoor temperature directly from the recent outdoor climate.

Why a second comfort model exists

Field studies in naturally ventilated buildings repeatedly found people comfortable at temperatures the heat-balance model called too warm. People in these buildings adapt: they change what they wear, open a window, slow down, and shift what they expect. The adaptive model captures that by making the comfort temperature a function of the outdoor climate the occupants have been living in.

Use this model only where occupants genuinely control the openings and there is no mechanical cooling in operation. In a sealed, air-conditioned building the PMV model is the right one.

Environmental factors

Prevailing mean outdoor temperature

The running mean of recent outdoor air temperature, accepted from 10 to 33.5 °C. It is the horizontal axis of the chart and the input that decides where the comfort bands sit: raise it and the whole comfort region moves to warmer indoor temperatures.

Note the name ASHRAE calls this the prevailing mean, EN 16798-1 calls its equivalent the running mean. The tool uses each standard's own term.

Indoor temperature

The condition being assessed, accepted from 10 to 40 °C. This model defaults to operative temperature rather than separate air and radiant values, since that is how the standard expresses the comfort relation. Separate entry is available if you have both measurements.

Air speed

Accepted from 0 to 2 m/s and offered with presets appropriate to a naturally ventilated space. Elevated air movement extends the acceptable range upward at warmer indoor temperatures.

Personal factors

None are entered. Metabolic rate and clothing insulation are not inputs to this model: its premise is that occupants adjust these themselves, so they are absorbed into the relation rather than supplied. Humidity is likewise not an input, for the same reason.

Outputs and the acceptability zones

The chart plots indoor operative temperature against prevailing mean outdoor temperature, with two bands drawn around the comfort temperature:

  • 80 % acceptability the wider band. This is the compliance criterion: a point inside it satisfies the standard.
  • 90 % acceptability the narrower band inside it, for spaces with a higher expectation of comfort.
Output Meaning
Comfort temperature The predicted comfortable operative temperature for the outdoor condition entered.
Zone Which band the assessed point falls in, or Too cool / Too warm outside them.
Compliance verdict Whether the point is inside the 80 % band.

Applicability

The relation is defined only over the outdoor temperature range above, and the chart is drawn over exactly that range. Outside it the standard makes no claim, and neither does the tool.

Why it is not in Explore

Explore lets you edit band edges as numbers. Adaptive bands are not numbers each edge is a function of the outdoor temperature, so it is a sloping line rather than a horizontal one. Allowing them to be dragged would produce a chart that looks like an adaptive assessment but is not one, so the model is offered only on its compliance workspace.

Where to find it

On the ASHRAE 55 workspace, alongside PMV. Switch between them with the model selector at the top of the input panel.
