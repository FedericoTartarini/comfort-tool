Models

Adaptive comfort (EN 16798-1)

adaptive-enEN 16798-1 workspaceNot in Explore

The European adaptive method. Like the ASHRAE adaptive model it predicts the comfortable indoor temperature from the recent outdoor climate, but it divides the result into three named building categories instead of two acceptability percentages.

Environmental factors

Running mean outdoor temperature

The exponentially weighted mean of recent daily outdoor temperature, and the horizontal axis of the chart.

Note the name: EN calls this the running mean, ASHRAE the prevailing mean. The tool uses each standard's own term, and the two relations are not interchangeable.

Indoor temperature

Accepted from 10 to 40 °C, entered as operative temperature by default.

Air speed

Accepted from 0 to 2 m/s, with presets suited to a naturally ventilated space. Air movement can widen the comfort region, allowing higher indoor operative temperatures.

Personal factors

None. As with the ASHRAE adaptive model, metabolic rate and clothing are not entered occupants of naturally conditioned buildings adjust these themselves.

The three categories

Category Intended for
Category I Spaces occupied by people with high expectations or particular sensitivity the narrowest band.
Category II New buildings and renovations; the normal design case.
Category III Existing buildings; a moderate, wider expectation.

The bands are nested: category I sits inside category II, which sits inside category III. The tool treats category III as the compliance criterion, so a point inside the widest band is reported as compliant, and the result panel also names the best category the point achieves. Points outside the widest band are reported as too cool or too warm.

Outputs

Output Meaning
Comfort temperature The predicted comfortable operative temperature for the outdoor condition entered.
Category The best category the assessed point achieves, or Too cool / Too warm.
Compliance verdict Whether the point is inside category III.

Applicability

The chart is drawn over a running mean outdoor temperature range of 10 °C to 30 °C, which is the range EN 16798-1 defines the relation over. This is deliberately narrower than the equivalent range in the ASHRAE adaptive model, so the same outdoor condition can be assessable under one standard and out of range under the other.

If you are comparing the two adaptive models, compare them at an outdoor temperature both cover. Their bands are differently derived and will not agree exactly even there, which is expected.

Why it is not in Explore

For the same reason as the ASHRAE adaptive model: its band edges are functions of outdoor temperature, not fixed numbers, and cannot meaningfully be edited.

Where to find it

On the EN 16798-1 workspace, which offers this model only.
