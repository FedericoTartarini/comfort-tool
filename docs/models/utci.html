UTCI Universal Thermal Climate Index

utciExplore onlyNo compliance workspace

UTCI expresses any combination of temperature, radiation, wind and humidity as the air temperature of a reference environment that would cause the same physiological strain. It is the standard index for outdoor thermal assessment, and it covers the whole climate range rather than the narrow indoor band the comfort models assume.

How to read a UTCI value

A UTCI of 28 °C does not mean the air is 28 °C. It means the conditions strain the body as much as 28 °C would in the reference environment calm air, moderate humidity, no extra radiation. That is what makes it comparable across a windy overcast day and a still sunny one.

Environmental factors

Input Accepted range Notes
Air temperature −50 to 50 °C The full outdoor climate range.
Mean radiant temperature −80 to 120 °C Wide, because a sunlit surface or one exposed to a clear night sky is far from the air temperature outdoors.
Wind speed 0 to 17 m/s Defined at 10 m above ground see the warning below.
Relative humidity 0 to 100 %
Wind is the input to be most careful with. UTCI is defined for wind measured 10 m above ground, which is what weather stations report. A value measured at head height in a sheltered courtyard is not the same number and will overstate comfort.

An operative-temperature entry mode is available, which sets radiant temperature equal to air temperature. That is a reasonable simplification in shade and a poor one in direct sun.

Personal factors

None. UTCI assumes a walking reference person whose clothing adapts to the temperature. That assumption is what makes one number comparable across climates and also what makes the index unsuitable for assessing a specific person in specific clothing.

Outputs: the ten stress categories

UTCI returns a single value, classified into ten categories of thermal stress:

Strong cold stress
Moderate cold stress
Slight cold stress		 No thermal stress Moderate heat stress
Strong heat stress

The categories, not the number, are what a UTCI assessment usually reports. The chart is drawn across the full index range roughly −50 to 55 °C with each category shaded, so a point's position gives you the value and the stress level at a glance.

Applicability

The input ranges above are the boundaries of the index's definition, and the tool enforces them. Outside them UTCI is not defined the model was fitted within that envelope, and an extrapolated number is not meaningful even if a formula returns one.

Where to find it

In Explore only. UTCI belongs to no building standard, so it has no compliance workspace and there is no pass or fail verdict to give only the value and its stress category.

Heat Index, Humidex and Wind Chill are also Explore-only for the same reason. UTCI is the most complete of the four: it accounts for radiation and wind together, where the others each look at one part of the problem.
