Models

Humidex

Not in v1. Humidex is not registered in rewrite/v1. The page below describes the archived refactor-draft interface, which this branch keeps as a record. It has not been rewritten. Verified against rewrite/v1 at 4d6f6df.

humidexExplore only2 inputs

Humidex is the Canadian equivalent of the Heat Index: a single number combining air temperature and humidity to express how hot it feels. It is derived from the dew point rather than from relative humidity directly, and it is banded into six discomfort levels.

Environmental factors

Input Accepted range
Air temperature 20 to 50 °C
Relative humidity 0 to 100 %

As with Heat Index, the lower temperature bound is part of the definition Humidex describes warm humid conditions and is not defined for cool ones.

Personal factors

None. Humidex assumes shade and still air, and represents no particular person.

Outputs and discomfort categories

A single Humidex value, classified into six levels:

Category
Little or no discomfort
Noticeable discomfort
Evident discomfort
Intense discomfort; avoid exertion
Dangerous discomfort
Heat stroke probable

The scale is a discomfort scale rather than a clinical risk scale, which is the main difference in tone from the Heat Index categories.

Charts

One chart: a banded field of air temperature against relative humidity, with axes fixed to the model's two inputs.

Humidex or Heat Index?

They answer the same question with different arithmetic and different category names, and they do not agree numerically a given condition produces different values under each. Use whichever your audience knows: Humidex in Canada, Heat Index in the United States. For anything requiring radiation and wind, use UTCI.

Applicability

An empirical index for warm humid air. The tool enforces the ranges above, and the value should not be extrapolated beyond them.

Where to find it

In Explore only.

References

  • Masterton, J.M. and Richardson, F.A. (1979). Humidex: a method of quantifying human discomfort due to excessive heat and humidity. Environment Canada.
